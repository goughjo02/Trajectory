# Interview guide

Choose prompts from the gaps in the person's story. This is a menu, not a questionnaire to exhaust. Usually ask one to three connected questions per turn, fewer when an answer is emotionally difficult or detailed.

## Optional jump start

“Do you have anything we could start from—a LinkedIn profile, CV, cover letter, website, portfolio, or rough notes? You can share links, files, or pasted text, or we can just talk.”

Use what is already supplied before asking this. Summarise the initial map and identify gaps, contradictions, and stories worth exploring. Ask what is outdated only where it matters. Material can arrive later or mid-story; incorporate it without restarting the interview.

## Direction and circumstances

What brings you here now? What would a better next chapter change? Is there anything time-sensitive? Discover practical constraints without assuming salary is the sole goal. Ask how they rank learning, stability, flexibility, location, income, responsibility, meaning, and other priorities they name. Separate needs from preferences and longer-term possibilities.

## Map the history

Invite a rough timeline of roles, study, independent projects, and other relevant experience. Existing CVs can supply dates and titles, but not assumed ownership or achievements. Mark approximate dates and gaps; do not demand explanations for breaks. Ask which chapters feel significant, overlooked, or hard to describe.

## Recall a role

Start with ordinary work: what did a typical week involve, who relied on them, what did they own? Then try a concrete doorway:

- A difficult day, an unusually satisfying result, or something others sought their help with.
- A recurring problem they noticed and changed; what happened before and after?
- Work that was invisible because they kept something running or prevented a problem.
- A disagreement, uncertainty, setback, recovery, handover, or person they helped develop.
- An artefact they can describe: a report, lesson, service, event, design, process, care plan, analysis, or product. Do not request confidential artefacts.

Follow the story, not every bullet. Establish the stakes, their specific decisions/actions, collaborators' contributions, constraints, outcome and evidence, and what they learned. Ask for magnitude only if they might know it. An honest qualitative outcome is useful; never manufacture a percentage. Distinguish correlation from demonstrated causation and team results from personal ownership.

If recall stalls, offer a different time period or category. If it still yields little, leave a revisit note and move on. Do not pressure someone to turn distress into an interview story.

## Culture, ambition, and trade-offs

Ask about a period when they did their best work and a contrasting environment that drained them. What changed: manager behaviour, colleagues, autonomy, feedback, pace, resources, ethics, predictability, or the work itself? Which conditions were temporary and which keep recurring?

Explore what they want more of, what they want to leave behind, and what they would like to be capable of in a few years. Ask what trade-off they would accept and what would make an attractive offer wrong for them. Ambivalence and evolving ambitions are valid.

Example of useful synthesis: “You enjoyed choosing the approach on that project, but valued close guidance when you moved into a new area. My tentative read is that you want autonomy with accessible support. Where does that fit, and where does it miss?” Do not store this example as the user's preference.

Tie hypotheses to decisions, such as manager questions or role criteria. If an inference would not change career advice, it probably does not need recording. Keep rejected interpretations rejected rather than returning to them later.

## Voice and positioning

Use the person's actual language. Ask what they wish people understood about their work, what they dislike in professional writing, and whether a short sample sounds like them. A polished draft must not become a source of new factual claims. Allow uncertainty about direction and multiple plausible paths.
